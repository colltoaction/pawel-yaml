# 13. Ed Fredkin

**Speaker**: Ed Fredkin  
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0uLGRj2wLFs
**Duration**: 8 minutes, 21 seconds  
**Channel**: Bruce Baumgart

---

Ed Fredkin shares anecdotes about his 55-year friendship with John McCarthy, highlighting McCarthy's intellectual prowess and pioneering work in computer science. Fredkin recounts their early interactions concerning "tree memory" and LISP, McCarthy's spontaneous proof of a difficult problem during a lecture, and his groundbreaking concept of time-sharing. The talk also touches on their shared interest in influencing events in the Soviet Union during the Cold War, including McCarthy's efforts to free Andrei Sakharov. Fredkin concludes by praising McCarthy as a brilliant American hero who combined theory and practicality to shape the future.

Here's a breakdown of the key information:

1957 - Tree Memory and First Meeting with John McCarthy (0:04-1:45): Fredkin, an Air Force pilot at MIT's Lincoln Laboratory, sought McCarthy's opinion on his "tree memory" idea. McCarthy initially seemed annoyed but ultimately confirmed the originality of Fredkin's concept, marking the beginning of their friendship.
McCarthy's Spontaneous Proof during a Lecture (1:48-2:42): Fredkin recounts attending McCarthy's lecture where McCarthy, without preparation, spontaneously solved a difficult open question about program-proving on the blackboard, demonstrating his brilliant mind.
The Universe as a Program - Philosophical Discussion (2:44-3:31): While working at BBN, Fredkin discussed his idea of the universe being a program with McCarthy, who had a similar concept, though with different ideas about detecting "roundoff or truncation errors." McCarthy encouraged Fredkin to continue exploring these ideas.
Development of Time-Sharing (3:35-4:22): Fredkin highlights McCarthy's development of time-sharing, a concept that allowed multiple users to interact simultaneously with a mainframe computer. Fredkin, as a hardware designer, was inspired to help implement this idea.
McCarthy's Move to Stanford and MIT's Oversight (4:25-5:16): Fredkin recounts McCarthy's plan to leave MIT after not being promoted, and his subsequent move to Stanford after Caltech showed no interest. Fredkin expresses gratitude to Stanford for recognizing McCarthy's talent.
Influencing Events in the Soviet Union and Prague Spring (5:18-6:44): Both Fredkin and McCarthy had contacts in the Soviet Union and tried to influence events, sharing an optimistic view of communism transitioning to democracy. Fredkin describes McCarthy's urgent telegram to Moscow threatening to cut off his visit if they invaded Czechoslovakia during the Prague Spring.
Problem-Solving Course and Doctor's Dilemma (6:46-7:12): McCarthy contributed problems for Fredkin's problem-solving course at MIT, including "the doctor's dilemma" and a problem related to the US bicentennial.
Freeing Andrei Sakharov (7:14-7:44): Fredkin shares how McCarthy, through his connection with Vice President Bush, relayed Fredkin's unusual discussions about Andrei Sakharov during a USSR visit, ultimately influencing Russia to free Sakharov from exile.
John McCarthy: A Brilliant American Hero (7:46-8:19): Fredkin concludes by eulogizing McCarthy as a brilliant American hero, emphasizing his unique combination of theory and practicality, his foresight, and his role in shaping the future, and cherishing their 55 years of friendship.
