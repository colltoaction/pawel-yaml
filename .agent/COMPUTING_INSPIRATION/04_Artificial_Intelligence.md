# 4. Artificial Intelligence - John McCarthy

**Speaker**: John McCarthy (1927-2011)  
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ozipf13jRr4
**Duration**: 27 minutes, 32 seconds  
**Channel**: ThinkingAllowedTV

---

This video features an interview with Dr. John McCarthy, a pioneer in Artificial Intelligence (AI). He discusses the history and future of AI, reflecting on its progress and the significant conceptual challenges that remain. McCarthy emphasizes the importance of non-monotonic reasoning in enabling computers to simulate human-like common sense and intelligence, and he shares his optimistic outlook on the future of AI despite its difficulties.

Here are the key points from the discussion:

Introduction of Dr. John McCarthy: (0:42) The video introduces Dr. John McCarthy as a founder of AI, inventor of Lisp, conceiver of interactive computer time-sharing, developer of non-monotonic reasoning, and recipient of the Kyoto Prize.
Reflections on AI Progress: (3:02) McCarthy notes that AI has made less progress than he initially hoped due to difficult conceptual problems, particularly in solving problems related to common sense.
The Challenge of Common Sense: (4:45) He highlights that simple tasks for humans, like speech recognition or common-sense reasoning, have been the most challenging for AI, contrasting with the early success in complex mathematical problems.
Computer Science Approach to AI: (6:11) McCarthy explains that the computer science approach focuses on identifying problems and achieving goals, rather than merely imitating human biology or psychology.
AI's Impact on Psychology: (6:58) He discusses how AI and computer science helped psychology move away from behaviorism by demonstrating the need to understand internal information processing.
Self-Consciousness in AI: (9:01) The discussion touches on the complex problem of programming self-consciousness in computers, which involves regarding the self as an object in the world and tracking progress toward goals.
Turing Test and Philosophical Issues: (9:54) McCarthy addresses Alan Turing's philosophical arguments about machine intelligence and the Turing test, acknowledging ongoing debates about whether computers can truly "think."
Critique of Consciousness as Sum of Parts: (11:57) He refutes the idea that consciousness is merely the sum of its parts, arguing that a system requires specified connections and interactions.
Optimism Despite Difficulties: (13:13) McCarthy maintains an optimistic outlook, believing that challenging aspects of human consciousness not yet replicated in machines will eventually be realized.
Chess Program Bet: (14:17) He recounts a bet made in 1968 that a computer would beat a chess master within 10 years, noting that a computer program eventually defeated a grandmaster, though often through "brute force" calculation.
Importance of Non-Monotonic Reasoning: (15:56) McCarthy's work aims to formalize non-monotonic reasoning to enable machines to use mental shortcuts and common-sense rules, rather than relying solely on brute-force computation.
Explanation of Non-Monotonic Reasoning: (17:40) He clarifies that non-monotonic reasoning is a logical form where conclusions can be retracted when new information is added, using the example of a bird that may or may not fly depending on additional facts (e.g., being a penguin or having its feet encased in cement).
Simplicity and Difficulty of AI Breakthroughs: (22:26) McCarthy suggests that future breakthroughs in AI, particularly regarding common sense, will seem simple in hindsight, much like fundamental logical concepts took centuries to formalize.
Future of AI: (25:11) He avoids specific timelines but bets on AI achieving human-level intelligence within approximately 50 years, expressing confidence against critics who deem it theoretically impossible.
AI as "Know Thyself": (26:38) McCarthy concludes by linking the AI project to the Socratic injunction, "Know Thyself," viewing it as a noble and challenging human endeavor.
