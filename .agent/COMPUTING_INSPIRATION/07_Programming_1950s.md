# 7. Programming in the 1950s

**Speaker**: John Backus  
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EylBknGtkqA
**Duration**: 34 minutes, 34 seconds  
**Channel**: Computer History Museum

---

John Backus, a key figure in the development of the Fortran programming language, shares his personal impressions of programming in the 1950s. He emphasizes that early programming was challenging yet enjoyable, requiring ingenuity due to limited machine capabilities and lack of established principles. He recounts an amusing anecdote about programming the SSEC, highlighting the bizarre issues programmers faced. Backus also discusses the shift in the programmer's role from an "artist" to one constrained by manuals, and the resistance from a "priesthood" of programmers who opposed making programming more accessible. He touches upon the historical obscurity of early elegant systems like the one by Lanning and Zierler, contrasting it with the wider adoption of less elegant but more widely available systems like Speedcoding. Finally, he elaborates on the Fortran project's focus on producing efficient object programs and acknowledges the significant contributions of Robert Nelson, Irving Ziller, and Sheldon Best in developing optimization techniques.

Here are the key points that lead to the summary:

Early Programming Challenges and Fun: Backus highlights that programming in the early 1950s was fun due to the "absurd difficulties" presented by computers, which forced programmers to be resourceful inventors (2:59 - 3:27).
Limited Resources and Ingenuity: Programmers had to fit code into tiny stores, overcome bizarre difficulties with data input/output, use limited instruction sets, and employ every trick to make programs run fast due to high operational costs (3:27 - 3:59).
The SSEC Anecdote: An amusing story illustrates the extreme challenges, involving a huge intermediate data tape that was accidentally glued into a Mobius strip, causing mysterious errors (4:32 - 6:46).
Shift in Programmer's Role: Backus notes a change from programming as a "black art" to a more standardized process dictated by thick manuals (7:05 - 7:45).
"Priesthood" of Programmers: He describes a group of pioneering programmers who saw themselves as guardians of complex skills, often hostile towards efforts to make programming easier for a larger population (14:55 - 16:20).
Obscurity of Elegant Systems: Backus discusses how elegant early systems, like the algebraic compiler by Lanning and Zierler, remained obscure due to limited machine compatibility and resistance from the "priesthood" (17:48 - 19:36).
Fortran's Optimization Focus: The main problem for the Fortran project was producing efficient object programs, especially with the advent of the IBM 704 with built-in floating point (20:10 - 20:42).
Key Optimizers in Fortran: He specifically acknowledges Robert Nelson and Irving Ziller for devising methods to analyze and optimize loops and array references, and Sheldon Best for optimizing index register usage based on execution frequency (21:19 - 24:53).
