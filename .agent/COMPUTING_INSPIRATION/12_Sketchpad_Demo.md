# 12. Ivan Sutherland Sketchpad Demo

**Speaker**: Ivan Sutherland  
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6orsmFndx_o
**Duration**: 19 minutes, 20 seconds  
**Channel**: Interface Studies

---

This video from 1963 demonstrates Ivan Sutherland's Sketchpad program, a groundbreaking system for graphical man-machine communication developed at MIT's Lincoln Laboratory. The program allows users to interact with a computer by drawing directly on an oscilloscope screen using a light pen, enabling a more intuitive and cooperative design process than traditional methods.

Here's a breakdown of the key aspects discussed:

Introduction to Sketchpad (0:47-1:12): The hosts introduce the concept of "talking graphically" to a computer using a "graphical language" called Sketchpad, initiated by Ivan Sutherland.
New Approach to Problem Solving (1:20-2:35): The video highlights how Sketchpad differs from conventional computer use, allowing designers to investigate ideas interactively and the computer to act as an "intelligent assistant," rather than just a calculating machine requiring precise, pre-defined steps.
Graphical Communication with the Light Pen (4:17-4:37): The demonstration begins by showing how a light pen is used on an oscilloscope to draw information directly into the computer.
Drawing and Manipulating Objects (4:39-6:26): The demonstration shows the drawing of straight lines and circles, and how the computer understands the geometry, allowing users to move connected lines simultaneously when one point is adjusted.
Precision and "Gravity Fields" (5:27-5:53): The system's ability to snap the light pen's dot onto lines and endpoints (described as a "gravity field") is shown, allowing for precise drawings even with sloppy pen movements.
Deleting and Redrawing (6:38-7:07): The ease of deleting mistakes and redrawing shapes like circles by indicating a center and radius is demonstrated.
Constraints and Dynamic Adjustments (7:40-8:22): A key feature, the "constraint command," is shown, where the computer can automatically adjust a drawing to satisfy conditions, like making a circle end precisely on a line.
Master Drawings and Copies (8:30-9:57): The ability to save "master" drawings and create copies that can be manipulated (reduced, magnified, rotated) is explained, as well as the revolutionary feature of automatic updates across all copies when the master drawing is changed.
"Infinite" Paper and Zoom (10:12-11:27): The concept of the 7-inch scope as a "window" to an "approximately 2 miles on a side" virtual paper is introduced, showcasing the ability to zoom in and out dramatically, revealing details or the entire vast drawing space.
Three-Dimensional Capabilities (11:32-14:26): The expanded three-dimensional program is demonstrated, showing a 3D object from four views (top, front, side, perspective) and the ability to rotate, draw directly in 3D, and distort objects in real-time.
Solid Object Representation (14:42-16:26): Dr. Lawrence Roberts explains how the system represents solid objects with hidden lines, giving a realistic 3D feel as objects are rotated and intersected.
Flowcharting for Programming (16:40-17:32): The application of Sketchpad to flowcharting programs is shown, allowing programmers to draw diagrams of computer operations that can be directly compiled and executed.
Applying Constraints for Design Refinement (17:33-19:02): The video concludes by reiterating the design process, where users can start with a rough idea and apply constraints (like horizontal or vertical lines) later to refine the drawing into an exact shape, moving beyond a simple drafting machine.
